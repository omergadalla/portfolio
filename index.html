FLAGSHIP DEVELOPMENT

100N Liquid Bipropellant Engine

PHASE 01: SIMULATION

Flow Validation.

Before manufacturing, we had to prove the flow physics. I ran Viscous Compressible Flow simulations in ANSYS Fluent to validate our nozzle contour.

The goal was to prevent flow separation at Mach 3. Results matched our Isentropic Flow calculations within 5%.

> ANSYS Fluent / K-Epsilon Model
PHASE 02: THERMAL STRATEGY

Heat Sink Design.

A 10-second burn at 3000K generates immense thermal loads.

We utilized a Heat Sink / Capacitive cooling strategy. We selected high-performance Inconel and Copper alloys capable of absorbing the thermal load for the full duration without exceeding the material's structural failure limits.

> Transient Thermal Analysis / Inconel 718
PHASE 03: ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING

Printed for Precision.

To achieve the complex internal geometries of the injector and cooling interfaces, we moved away from traditional CNC.

The combustion chamber and injector were manufactured using Additive Manufacturing (3D Printing). This allowed us to print the complex swirler patterns directly in Inconel.

Propellants: Gaseous Oxygen (GOX) & Jet-A1

PHASE 04: VALIDATION

The Spray Test.

Before ignition, we verify fluid dynamics. We conducted a "Cold Flow" test to visualize the injector's atomization cone.

This confirmed that our GOX/Fuel mixture would impinge correctly, ensuring stable combustion and preventing hard starts.
