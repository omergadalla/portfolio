Technical Projects

FLAGSHIP PROJECT

100N Liquid Bipropellant Rocket Engine

From System Modeling to firing

PHASE 01: SYSTEM MODELING

Architecture & Logic.

We first did The NASA CEA then the simscape model of the design. This critical first step established our propellant mass flow rates and sizing requirements before any CAD geometry was generated.

> NASA CEA / MATLAB Simscape
PHASE 02: CFD SIMULATION

Flow Validation.

Before manufacturing, we had to prove the flow physics. I ran a CFD simulation in ANSYS Fluent to validate our nozzle contour. The goal was to investigate the shock location and nozzle flow properties.

> ANSYS Fluent
PHASE 03: THERMAL STRATEGY

Heat Sink Design.

A 10-second burn at 3000K generates immense thermal loads. We utilized a Heat Sink cooling strategy. We selected high-performance Inconel and Copper alloys capable of absorbing the thermal load for the full duration.

> Transient Thermal Analysis / Inconel 718
PHASE 04: ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING

Printed for Precision.

To achieve the complex internal geometries of the injector, we moved away from traditional CNC. The combustion chamber and injector were manufactured using Additive Manufacturing.

Propellants: Gaseous Oxygen (GOX) & Jet-A1

PHASE 05: VALIDATION

The Spray Test.

Before ignition, we verify fluid dynamics. We conducted a "Cold Flow" test to visualize the injector's atomization cone. This confirmed that our GOX/Fuel mixture would impinge correctly, ensuring stable combustion.
